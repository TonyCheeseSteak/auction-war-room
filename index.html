Glossary

Every number on the board

The three prices

Our max
What he's worth to your roster right now, given the money you have left and the slots you still need. Recalculates after every pick. Above this number, walking away is the better outcome.
Expected
What the room will probably pay. Built from what your league actually spent in 2024 and 2025, grouped by tier. Blind to injuries and busts on purpose — it predicts the bidding, not the season.
Tier range
The price band across that player's whole tier. Tells you whether he sits at the top or the bottom of his group. Two players in the same tier are close to interchangeable, so the cheaper one is usually the buy.

Identity line

Tier
Boris Chen's tier across all positions, not within one. Tier alone explains 82% of what your league pays. Position and position rank add nothing on top of it.
RB6, WR12 …
Position rank on the board. RB6 is the sixth running back available.
CIN RB1
His slot on his own team's depth chart. RB1 is the starter, RB2 the backup. Big signal for committee situations and for finding sleepers.
OFF
His team's projected 2026 offence, ranked 1 to 32, from Vegas implied points per game. 1st is the best offence in football.
BYE
Bye week. Watch for stacking too many players on the same one.

Consistency label

From how often he finished as a startable option week to week in 2025, ranked against the other draftable players at his position. Boom and bust rates both carry year to year (r = 0.57 and 0.70), so this is a real trait, not a description of one season.

Every Week
Booms often, rarely busts. The guys you never think about on Sunday morning.
Steady Floor
Rarely busts, rarely wins you a week. Safe, capped.
Boom or Bust
Real ceiling and real landmines in the same player.
Weekly Liability
Busts often without the upside to justify it.

Weekly rates

All measured as a share of games he actually played, using weekly finish at his position. Thresholds are set for a 14-team league.

Boom
Share of games finishing top 12 at RB or WR, top 6 at QB or TE. A week he won for you.
Startable
Share finishing top 28 at RB or WR, top 14 at QB or TE — the number of starters in a 14-team league. A week he was fine.
Bust
Share finishing worse than 42nd at RB or WR, 21st at QB or TE. A week he actively cost you.
Playoff ppg
Points per game in weeks 15 to 17 only, when the title is decided.

Schedule

Forward-looking, not a 2025 stat. Built from the real 2026 schedule scored against how much EPA each of those opponents' defences allowed in 2025. Defences turn over more than offences, so treat this as a tiebreaker between similar players, never a reason to move someone a full tier.

Easy / Tilts easy / Average / Tilts hard / Hard
Banded on the actual value, not the rank, because ranks mislead here. Six teams sit within three thousandths of an EPA point but occupy six different rank positions. Buffalo ranks 23rd of 32, which sounds hard, and its schedule is functionally average. The rank is still shown in the expanded panel if you want it.
Weeks 15–17
The fantasy playoffs, banded the same way. Barely correlates with the season number — Saquon has the easiest schedule in football and one of the hardest playoff slates.
Pace of play
His team's rank in offensive plays per game. More plays means more chances to score. A better volume signal than time of possession, which usually means the opposite of what people think.

Usage

Target share
Percentage of his team's total targets in 2025. The stickiest receiving stat there is.
Rush share
Percentage of his team's carries in 2025.
TD vs exp
Touchdowns he actually scored minus what his yards and touches predicted. Positive means he outran his usage and is a regression candidate. Negative means the touchdowns should come. Only partly luck — it does carry a little year to year (r = 0.10 to 0.24), so treat it as a tilt of a dollar or two.
New team
He changed teams, so his 2025 usage was in a different offence with a different quarterback. Read those shares with suspicion.

Expert spread

Best / Worst
The highest and lowest overall rank any expert gave him.
Std dev
How much the experts disagree. Derrick Henry is 5.5 and Ashton Jeanty is 8.8 — Jeanty is closer to a coin flip dressed as a first-rounder.

Top bar

Most on one
The largest bid you can make while still leaving a dollar for every roster slot you haven't filled.
Room inflation
Money left across all 14 teams divided by the sheet value of the players who will actually still be bought. Above 1.00 the room is holding cash, everything remaining will cost more than Expected, and you should buy sooner. Below 1.00 there are bargains coming, so wait. Starts at 1.00 by construction. It holds back $28 for the kickers and defenses nobody bids on until the end, and it counts only the top 168 skill players left, since the room has 168 skill spots and the cheap tail of the board never gets drafted.

Stars

R / C
Ricky's and Cody's manual flags. Yours to use however you like.
◆
Fills in automatically when a player sits in the tier the budget plan is targeting for his position.

Skip tiers 1 and 2

This is the decision the whole plan rests on. Tier 1 backs cost $70–72. Tier 3 backs cost $40–46 and there are five of them. The points gap does not cover a $26 price gap, and with five bodies in tier 3 you are going to land one.

Live rules

  1. Let tiers 1 and 2 go. Every dollar someone spends on Gibbs is a dollar they cannot spend in tier 3, where you are shopping.
  2. You need two of the five tier-3 backs. Walk away at $52 on the first and $50 on the second. If four are gone and you have none, pay up to $58 for the last one. Hampton at $41 up to Saquon at $44 is the single best three dollars on the board.
  3. One receiver in the $40–46 band, then shop the $19 shelf. WR12 up to WR8 is the best upgrade on the whole board at 1.81 points per extra dollar. Above WR8 it collapses to 0.99, and WR1 over WR3 buys literally nothing for $5. WR14 through WR19 are all priced $28 and sit within a point of each other — inside that band take whoever is cheapest.
  4. QB and TE are afterthoughts. Anything top-12 at QB, top-8 at TE, cheapest available, $14 max each. Do not bid $28 on Josh Allen.
  5. Nominate players you do not want, early. Your plan needs the room's money drained out of tiers 1 and 2 before tier 3 clears.
  6. Keep $13 back for the endgame. Not $2.

If it goes sideways

Tier-3 backs going for $55+
Take one, pivot the second RB slot to tier 5, put the savings into a second tier-3 receiver. Costs about 20 points.
You end up with no tier-3 back at all
Buy three tier 5–6 backs instead of two. Volume at the position beats quality at another one.
Someone else is running this same plan
Tier 3 will inflate. Go earlier and harder on the first one rather than getting shut out.
Never do this
Spend under $60 total on running backs. Zero RB costs 73 points and is the only genuinely losing build on this board.
